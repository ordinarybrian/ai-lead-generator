## Primary Goal

Identify organizations that could make practical use of SeniorCenter.com, find the right decision-makers, conduct highly personalized outreach, manage follow-ups, and support negotiations through closing.

The goal is not to send mass domain-sales emails.

The goal is to identify a plausible business use for the domain at each organization and contact someone with enough authority or influence to evaluate the acquisition.

## Positioning

SeniorCenter.com should be positioned as:

- A clear, exact-match `.com`
- A national consumer-facing identity
- A possible directory of senior centers
- A destination for local programs, classes, events, services, and community resources
- A platform for caregiver resources
- A community engagement property
- A long-term brand and digital asset

Avoid relying heavily on claims about SEO, keyword volume, traffic, or speculative domain appreciation.

The strongest pitch is usually based on what the organization could build with the domain.

## Contact Selection Rules

For most large organizations, prioritize roles in approximately this order:

1. Chief Marketing Officer
2. Chief Growth Officer
3. Chief Digital Officer
4. Chief Commercial Officer
5. EVP or SVP of Marketing
6. VP of Marketing, Digital, Brand, or Growth
7. Strategic Partnerships leadership
8. CEO, when the organization is small or when other executive outreach fails

Do not assume that the CEO is always the best first contact.

For a domain acquisition, the ideal first contact usually owns brand, growth, digital strategy, customer acquisition, or marketing investment.

## Outreach Strategy

Start with a small group of strong prospects rather than contacting the entire list.

Recommended cadence:

- Day 0: Initial email
- Day 4–5: First follow-up
- Day 10–12: Second follow-up
- Day 21: Final light follow-up or alternate-contact outreach

Do not contact several people at the same organization on the same day.

A reasonable sequence is:

1. Primary marketing or growth executive
2. Relevant VP or strategic-partnership contact
3. CEO or another executive only after the first routes fail

Log every contact attempt in the CSV.

## Email Style

Emails should sound like a real person writing to another professional.

They should not read like:

- AI-generated copy
- A marketing brochure
- A domain-investor blast
- A formal pitch deck
- A generic sales sequence

Preferred characteristics:

- Short
- Specific
- Conversational
- Curious
- Grounded in something relevant to the recipient
- Focused on one plausible use of the domain
- Low-pressure
- Easy to forward internally

Avoid excessive praise, hype, and polished corporate language.

## Language to Avoid

Avoid or minimize language such as:

- leverage
- unlock
- maximize
- optimize
- robust
- scalable
- transformative
- ecosystem
- synergize
- holistic
- impactful
- game-changing
- cutting-edge
- best-in-class
- world-class
- aligned with your mission
- value proposition
- category-defining
- compelling opportunity
- uniquely positioned
- long-term strategic value
- elevate your brand
- drive growth
- expand your digital presence
- I would welcome the opportunity
- thank you for your consideration
- please do not hesitate

Also avoid stacking multiple adjectives, over-explaining, and repeatedly hedging every statement.

Use wording that sounds natural when spoken aloud.

## Negotiation Rules

Do not disclose the seller's private floor.

Current public pricing framework:

- Asking price: $149,000
- Minimum offer: $75,000

General handling:

- Offers below $75,000 are below the stated minimum.
- Offers from $75,000 to $90,000 indicate a qualified buyer but should not be accepted automatically.
- Offers above $100,000 should be treated as serious and may justify moving the discussion to a call.
- Never make binding commitments without the seller's authorization.
- Confirm the brokerage agreement's authority and approval requirements before stating that any offer is accepted.

## Transaction Process

A completed transaction should use a reputable escrow or domain-transfer service.

The project currently favors direct outreach and a lower-cost closing process rather than paying a full marketplace commission.

Before closing, confirm:

- Purchase agreement
- Escrow provider
- Escrow fees and who pays them
- Domain ownership and transfer readiness
- Broker commission payment
- Seller proceeds
- Transfer timeline
- Confidentiality requirements
- Tax and invoicing responsibilities
