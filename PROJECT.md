# Project Context

This is the one file you edit to stand up a new lead-generation project on this framework.
Fill in every bracketed placeholder. Delete a section only if it truly doesn't apply — the
"Optional commercial terms" section is the one most likely to be skippable.

Each section below includes a short inline example showing how it might be filled in for
a domain sale, a job search, and a B2B partnership push, so the pattern is clear even
though your project is probably none of those three exactly.

## What Is Being Offered or Sought

One paragraph. What is this project trying to place with someone else — a product, an
asset, a candidate's labor, a partnership, a donation ask? State it plainly.

> This project is trying to establish partnerships and referral relationships for Inland Elite Hosting, LLC, primarily with freelance web designers, developers, marketing professionals, and small agencies in North Idaho (Coeur d'Alene, Post Falls, Sandpoint) and the Spokane area. Inland Elite provides managed WordPress hosting, website maintenance, migrations, and ongoing technical support, giving these partners a dependable place to send clients when they don't want to manage hosting infrastructure or provide long-term technical support themselves. The goal is not to take over their design or marketing relationships, but to become a trusted local hosting and technical partner who can receive referrals, handle post-launch website care, or work behind the scenes alongside them.

## Primary Goal

What does this project exist to accomplish, and what does "success" look like at the end
of it — a signed deal, an accepted offer, a scheduled pilot, a grant awarded?

> This project exists to build a repeatable client-acquisition channel for Inland Elite Hosting, LLC by identifying and developing relationships with web designers, freelancers, developers, and marketing agencies that need a reliable partner for hosting and ongoing website support. Success is a working referral or partnership relationship in which a prospect agrees to recommend Inland Elite to appropriate clients, hand off hosting and maintenance accounts, or use Inland Elite as a behind-the-scenes technical resource. The immediate measurable outcome is a qualified conversation that progresses to an agreed partnership arrangement; the ultimate outcome is new recurring hosting and managed-service clients generated through those relationships.

> The outreach should be framed as a professional partnership, not a sales pitch: Inland Elite Hosting exists to take hosting, WordPress maintenance, migrations, and ongoing technical support off the prospect’s plate when those responsibilities are outside their expertise, unprofitable, or simply work they would rather not manage. ## Target Categories

Who counts as a qualified lead for this project? List the categories worth researching,
roughly in priority order.

> Small marketing agencies — Agencies offering web design/development, SEO, PPC, social media, or branding where hosting isn't prominently offered. Higher potential client volume than freelancers, although they're more likely to already have a hosting arrangement.
> Small branding and creative studios — Especially 1–5 person firms that offer websites alongside branding, graphic design, photography, or content. Hosting is often necessary to their work but isn't their core business.
> Independent marketing/SEO consultants — Particularly consultants working with WordPress sites. Site speed, uptime, security, and technical maintenance directly affect their work, but they may not want to provide those services themselves.
> Freelance web/graphic designers who build websites — Best fit, especially designers using WordPress who don't advertise their own hosting or managed maintenance. They have direct client relationships but may not want the technical responsibility after launch.
> Web developers who don't want to operate hosting infrastructure — Freelance developers and small development shops that build sites but currently send clients to national hosting companies or leave hosting decisions to the client.
> Graphic designers and brand designers who occasionally build websites — Lower website volume, but potentially an excellent fit because they're less likely to want responsibility for DNS, migrations, backups, WordPress updates, or troubleshooting.
> Advertising/PR agencies with web capabilities — Potentially significant client volume, but larger agencies are more likely to have established hosting/development relationships. Most attractive when Inland Elite can take smaller or legacy accounts off their hands.
> IT/MSP companies that don't specialize in websites — They already serve small businesses and are frequently asked about websites, domains, WordPress, and hosting. Inland Elite could become their website counterpart while they retain IT responsibility.
> Business consultants and fractional marketing professionals — They advise small businesses and may influence vendor selection, but website needs arise less consistently than with designers and agencies.
> Adjacent creative professionals — Photographers, copywriters, sign companies, print shops, and similar businesses that routinely work with small-business owners. These are worth cultivating eventually, but I'd prioritize them only after exhausting the more direct channels above.

## Contact-Selection Priority Order

For a typical target organization, who should be contacted first? List roles in priority
order. Note any small-organization exception (e.g., go straight to the CEO when no other
role exists).

1. Owner / Founder / Principal — Best first contact for freelancers, studios, and small agencies. They usually control vendor relationships, partnerships, and decisions about what happens to clients after launch.
2. Web Director / Director of Web Development / Development Lead — Best contact at a somewhat larger agency because they directly experience the burden of hosting, maintenance, migrations, and post-launch support.
3. Creative Director / Digital Director — Strong target at design- or marketing-led agencies, particularly when they oversee website projects but aren't responsible for infrastructure themselves.
4. Account Director / Client Services Director — Useful when this person controls ongoing client relationships and decides where work outside the agency's scope gets referred.
5. Operations Director / COO — Relevant at larger agencies where the hosting proposition is about reducing low-value support work 1. and operational overhead.
6. President / CEO — Appropriate for larger organizations if no more directly relevant decision-maker can be identified.

## Optional: Commercial Terms

**OPTIONAL — delete this section entirely if the project has no price, budget, salary,
or compensation component (e.g., most job searches, many partnership or fundraising
projects).**

> PRICE / BUDGET / COMPENSATION FIGURES: Inland Elite Hosting's standard retail pricing is $29/month Standard Hosting, $59/month Managed Hosting, $99/month Premium Hosting, and Private Server Hosting starting at $179/month. Custom development and technical work is billed at $90/hour. Partnership/referral compensation has not yet been established and should not be invented during outreach.

> WHITE-LABEL / RESELLER OPTION: For agencies and freelancers specifically, Inland Elite is open to a white-label arrangement: the partner provides and bills hosting to their own clients under their own brand or invoicing, sets their own markup, and Inland Elite operates the underlying infrastructure and technical support behind the scenes. This is a real, standing option and can be raised as a possibility when talking with agencies or freelancers, particularly ones that already sell their own hosting or maintenance retainer (where a pure referral pitch would otherwise compete with their existing offering). Specific wholesale/white-label pricing and markup terms have not been established and should not be invented during outreach. Raise it as an option to discuss, not a fixed program with set rates.

> MINIMUM ACCEPTABLE OUTCOME: There is no minimum monetary value required from an individual prospect. A successful initial outcome is a genuine partnership/referral relationship that has a reasonable chance of producing recurring hosting or managed-service clients. Do not discount services simply to secure a partnership without prior approval.

> CONFIDENTIAL — NEVER DISCLOSE OR INFER: Internal hosting/server costs, margins, profitability, existing client revenue, minimum acceptable margins, client-acquisition targets, capacity constraints, the owner's other employment/work schedule, and any future referral commission or partner-pricing limits established internally. Public retail pricing may be disclosed normally; internal economics and negotiation floors should not be.

## Outreach Cadence

> Day 0: Initial contact — Send a short, personalized email introducing Inland Elite Hosting and explaining the potential fit: you handle hosting, migrations, WordPress maintenance, and ongoing technical support for clients they don't want to support themselves. Reference something specific about their business or services. The goal is to start a conversation, not pitch a formal partnership.

> Day 5–7: First follow-up — Reply to the original email with a brief, low-pressure follow-up. Reinforce the core idea that you're looking to become a useful local technical resource rather than compete for their design, development, or marketing clients. Don't resend the entire pitch.

> Day 14–17: Second follow-up — Send one final substantive follow-up. Give them an easy reason to respond, such as asking how they currently handle hosting and post-launch support or whether they ever have clients they'd prefer to hand off. Keep it conversational and make clear there's no obligation to establish a formal partnership.

> Day 30+: Final touch or alternate contact — If there has been no response, stop actively pursuing the original contact. For an agency with another clearly relevant decision-maker, make one separate introduction to that person rather than forwarding the unanswered email chain. For freelancers and very small organizations, don't pursue another contact. Move the prospect into a long-term nurture list and only contact them again when there's a legitimate reason—such as a local introduction, relevant service update, or something specific to their business.

Do not contact multiple people at the same organization on the same day. Log every
contact attempt in `leads/lead-index.csv`, including a `next_action_date` for whatever
comes next so due and overdue follow-ups can be found without re-reading every row.

If a contact asks not to be contacted again, set `do_not_contact` to `TRUE` on their row
immediately — see `leads/README.md` and `AGENTS.md` for how that's enforced.

## Working Style

- Brian is based in Hayden, ID, and services North Idaho (Coeur d'Alene, Post Falls, Sandpoint, Hayden) and the Spokane area broadly. Don't state a specific home city (e.g. "I'm in Post Falls") in outreach. Use general regional language instead ("North Idaho," "the Spokane/Coeur d'Alene area").
- Make the smallest well-supported change needed and summarize what changed.
- Ask before sending outreach, publishing content, changing live systems, disclosing
  confidential information, or materially changing pricing/compensation/negotiation
  strategy.
- If uncertainty remains, label it plainly instead of filling gaps with assumptions.
