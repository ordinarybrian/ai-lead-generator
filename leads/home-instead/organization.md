# Home Instead

## Organization

- Organization: Home Instead (branded "Home Instead, an Honor Company")
- Website: https://www.homeinstead.com/
- Parent company: Honor Technology, Inc. (honorcare.com) acquired Home Instead, Inc. on August 6, 2021, combining Honor's caregiving technology/operations platform with Home Instead's franchise network.
- Organization type: For-profit, VC-backed home-care company. World's largest home-care franchise network, operating under the Home Instead brand as a subsidiary of Honor. Founded 1994 by Paul and Lori Hogan; began franchising in 1995.
- Scale: Reported in company materials as roughly 1,100+ locations across 13 countries (some sources say ~1,200 locations in 6 countries — figures vary by source and date; not independently reconciled) and 100,000+ professional caregivers. Combined Honor + Home Instead revenue has been cited at roughly $2.1 billion (2021, at time of acquisition) and other sources cite ~$9.5B in system-wide revenue for Home Instead specifically — treat these figures as approximate, unverified for current year.
- Mission (as stated in company materials): "To enhance the lives of aging adults and their families." The company positions itself around helping older adults live safely, independently, and comfortably at home.
- Relevant audience/market: Older adults who want to remain at home ("aging in place"), their family caregivers, and the network of local Home Instead franchise offices that deliver in-home care (companionship, personal care, meal prep, transportation, household help, Alzheimer's/dementia care).

## Corporate/brand context

- Honor is the technology and operations parent; Home Instead is the consumer-facing care brand. Honor's CEO is Seth Sternberg (co-founder); Home Instead's UK operation has a separate Managing Director (Ruth Brown) reporting into the same leadership team.
- Since 2023, Home Instead/Honor has been running a brand relaunch effort (per Chief Communications Officer Kim Atkinson's public statements) — repositioning the ~30-year-old Home Instead brand for the "global aging population."
- Home Instead has a "Chief Advocacy Officer" function reported to be working on a national strategy for family caregivers (mentioned in recent search results; name and full scope not yet verified — see sources.md and cautions).
- Post-acquisition, Home Instead franchisees formed an independent association (Independent Association of Home Instead Franchisees), reportedly representing 60%+ of U.S. system revenue, after friction with Honor over integration — worth being aware of as internal complexity, not something to reference in outreach.

## Potential SeniorCenter.com use

Home Instead is a home-care company, not a center-based one, so the fit is less literal than for an organization that runs physical senior centers — but there are two plausible hooks:

1. **Caregiver-resource / content hub angle.** Home Instead already publishes caregiver resources, care-planning content, and local "news and media" pages per franchise territory. SeniorCenter.com could function as a consumer-facing branded destination for caregiver resources, community programs, and local information — adjacent to, not competing with, their core in-home care service.
2. **National consumer acquisition / top-of-funnel brand angle.** Honor's business model already depends on a consumer-facing digital front door connecting families to local care. An exact-match, easy-to-remember domain like SeniorCenter.com is a plausible strategic asset for that same purpose, especially amid the active brand relaunch.

This is a working strategic hypothesis, not a claim about Home Instead's or Honor's current plans. The fit is weaker than for a literal "senior centers directory" buyer (e.g., AARP, a Medicare/health plan, or a senior-living operator) and should be pitched accordingly — as a content/community/brand asset, not as a literal match to their home-care service line.
