## Services and positioning — critical fit finding

**Front ROI is not primarily a website-design shop — it is an "all-in-one business growth" software platform (branded on-page as "Out Front CRM"), structurally similar to a white-labeled GoHighLevel-style CRM/marketing-automation reseller.** Per the company's own site:
- Core offering: CRM and lead management, SMS/email/voicemail/messenger campaigns, sales pipelines/proposals/contracts/invoicing/payments, social media planning, reputation management, workflow automation — **and** website design, listed as one bundled capability among many, not a standalone service.
- The dedicated `/website-design` page describes "custom" sites that are "fast, optimized, mobile-friendly, and fully integrated with your CRM," explicitly says the sites use **"lightweight, secure hosting optimized for performance"** (hosting is described as bundled/included, not a separate line item), and are "fully integrated with Front ROI's own platform, including forms, automations, SMS & email marketing, sales pipelines, and reputation management tools." Framing line from that page: "it's more than a website — it's your entire business system in one."
- No pricing is published; the site funnels visitors to a "Let's Talk" contact form / strategy call rather than displaying packages.
- No mention of WordPress (or any named CMS) anywhere reviewed — websites appear to be built and hosted inside Front ROI's own proprietary/platform stack, not on an open CMS.

## Why this is likely not a fit

- This is the same disqualifying pattern documented for AllerGale Design: **hosting is already bundled into a monetized platform the business operates itself**, not an occasional add-on or something they'd rather hand off. A referral/partnership pitch would ask Front ROI to send hosting business to Inland Elite when their own product already includes hosting as part of the sale.
- Beyond the hosting overlap, the underlying platform is proprietary/CRM-integrated rather than WordPress — a structural platform mismatch on top of the competing-product issue, comparable to AllerGale's Pixelcactus/Lotzabuzz situation.
- Front ROI is best understood as a full digital-marketing/CRM agency, not a solo or small freelance web designer — a weaker match to PROJECT.md's best-fit categories (freelance/small designer without existing hosting) even before the platform issue.
- The standing white-label option (PROJECT.md's "Optional Commercial Terms") is a weak fit here for the same reason as AllerGale: Front ROI isn't reselling someone else's hosting that could be swapped out — hosting appears to be built into their own platform's website-design feature.
