## Platform and hosting posture — fit finding

**Mixed-platform shop, not WordPress-exclusive — a partial fit rather than a clean match. No evidence found that Axess sells its own hosting; it does sell a $99/month content-maintenance retainer, but explicitly defers hosting/CMS subscriptions to the client.**

- Per the company's own FAQ page (axesswebdesign.com/web-design-faq/, retrieved 2026-08-20): "WordPress offers the most flexibility and is best for SEO-heavy or growing sites," while "Wix and Squarespace are great for simpler sites you want to manage easily yourself." The site also lists experience with Weebly and Shopify. This is a genuinely mixed-platform practice, not a WordPress specialist — a structural partial mismatch with Inland Elite's WordPress-only hosting specialty, though a meaningful share of their work does appear to be WordPress.
- Hosting: **no page reviewed mentions Axess selling or providing hosting.** The FAQ page explicitly frames domain/hosting as something arranged through "your CMS provider" — e.g., "Usually you can find a good deal through your CMS provider" for a matching custom email domain — consistent with clients managing their own hosting/domain rather than Axess hosting on their behalf. This is a favorable signal: hosting is treated as the client's (or the CMS platform's) responsibility, not something Axess already monetizes.
- Maintenance: Axess sells a **"Client Care Package" at $99/month** (per /website-design/, retrieved 2026-08-20) covering one blog post, one social media post, website edits, plugin updates, SSL renewal, one Google Business post, and a Google Analytics report per month — and will "purchase and register the domain name for you as part of the monthly Care Plan Package." This is a content/maintenance retainer, not a hosting product; it's an existing service line to be aware of when framing outreach (a pure "let us take maintenance off your plate" pitch would compete with the Care Package specifically, though a hosting/infrastructure-only pitch would not).

## Why this is a partial/plausible fit

- Positive: solo operator, no evidence of selling hosting, explicitly frames hosting/CMS as the client's or platform's responsibility, and a real share of client sites is WordPress.
- Caution: platform mix includes non-WordPress builds (Wix, Squarespace, Weebly, Shopify), so Inland Elite's hosting pitch would only be directly relevant to the WordPress subset of their client base, and the existing $99/mo Care Package is an adjacent (not identical) service worth being aware of, not framing a competing "maintenance" pitch against.
