## Hosting and maintenance — critical fit finding

**Victory Alliance Marketing explicitly sells managed WordPress hosting as part of its own website-maintenance service line. This is a direct, disqualifying overlap with Inland Elite's core offering, not just an adjacent maintenance retainer.**

- The company's own /website-maintenance/ page (victoryalliance.us/website-maintenance/, retrieved 2026-08-20) states: **"Yes, we provide managed WordPress hosting across all plans."** It describes "ongoing maintenance services designed for businesses, bloggers, and agencies" including updates, optimization, and support "across maintenance plans," and separately describes Shopify maintenance plans covering "product updates, app management, performance checks, bug fixes, and ongoing support." No specific pricing/tier names were visible on this page as fetched.
- This is a first-party statement, not an aggregator inference — Victory Alliance is not merely reselling third-party hosting or leaving hosting to the client; they present managed WordPress hosting as a standard, bundled part of every maintenance plan they sell.
- Comparable in kind to the AllerGale Design and Enleaf disqualification pattern (an agency that already operates its own monetized hosting/maintenance product), though narrower in scope than AllerGale's proprietary-CMS platforms — Victory Alliance's underlying stack is still WordPress/Shopify, not a proprietary CMS, so a backend white-label conversation is theoretically not impossible, but a straightforward referral pitch would ask them to send hosting clients to a competitor of a product they already sell under their own name.

## Why this is not a plausible fit for a referral pitch

- PROJECT.md's ideal freelance-designer/agency target is one where "hosting isn't prominently offered" and infrastructure is a burden they'd rather hand off. Victory Alliance is the opposite on this point: managed WordPress hosting is explicitly marketed as included in "all plans" of their maintenance service.
- A pure referral/partnership pitch (the primary ask per PROJECT.md) would ask them to send hosting clients to a direct competitor of an existing revenue line. The standing white-label option (PROJECT.md's "Optional Commercial Terms" section) is a possible angle if this lead is revisited — since the stack is WordPress, not proprietary — but it was not pursued further here given the small size of the business (~2 employees) and the direct nature of the overlap.
