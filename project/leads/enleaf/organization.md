# Enleaf

## Organization

- Organization: Enleaf (digital marketing agency)
- Website: https://enleaf.com/
- Organization type: Established digital marketing agency — SEO, PPC, web design, branding, and social media — headquartered in Spokane with a local office in Coeur d'Alene.
- HQ: 4249 E Pratt Ave Suite B, Spokane, WA 99202. Confirmed local Coeur d'Alene office on West Seltice Way ("we keep a local office in Coeur d'Alene, so we understand the local market" — per enleaf.com/marketing-agency-coeur-d-alene/).
- Founded 2009 — described on-site as "17+ years old" as of this research (2026-08-19).
- Additional location landing pages exist for Liberty Lake, Yakima, Seattle, Tacoma, Bellingham, Renton, and Cle Elum, WA. Site copy describes it as "a virtual agency with team members across the US and abroad," so most of these likely reflect SEO-targeted service-area pages rather than staffed physical branch offices — Coeur d'Alene is the one location independently confirmed to have a real local office address.

## Services and positioning

- Core services: web design (WordPress, Shopify, and custom builds), SEO (including local SEO), PPC/Google Ads, branding and logo design, social media marketing, and LinkedIn management.
- Self-described as "an award-winning digital marketing agency," "Top 10 Marketing Agency in the Pacific Northwest," serving clients from small businesses to Fortune 1000 companies, across sectors including real estate, travel/hospitality, e-commerce, SaaS, contracting, industrial, and manufacturing.
- Founder/CEO Adam Chronister has a high public profile: author of *Authority Engineering: How to Become the Brand AI Recommends*, a conference speaker, and the subject of multiple press releases/podcast features on AI-driven marketing (2025–2026).

## Hosting posture — competing in-house offering (important)

- Enleaf sells its own web hosting and maintenance, bundled with its WordPress design service — this is a real, currently-marketed in-house offering, not just a search snippet claim:
  - From enleaf.com/services/wordpress-website-design/: "Reliable, fast WordPress hosting with page speed control and 24/7 monitoring so your site stays online and loads quickly for every visitor," and a maintenance plan covering "regular backups, plugin and core updates, and proactive security."
  - Same page states the team "blends WordPress development, web design, SEO, hosting, plugins, and ongoing support under one roof."
  - Search-aggregated pricing color (not independently fetched from a pricing page): maintenance retainer "on average... about $50/month."
- Practical implication: a pure referral pitch ("send us your hosting/maintenance clients") would directly compete with an existing Enleaf revenue line, not fill a gap. The only positioning likely to land here is the white-label/reseller angle from PROJECT.md's commercial-terms section (relevant precisely because they already sell their own hosting/maintenance retainer) — not the standard freelancer-handoff pitch.

## Team size

- Sources disagree and none are primary/authoritative: RocketReach lists 6 employees; TechBehemoths lists 2; RankWatch lists "2-10"; TheOrg's org chart lists "1-10" with Adam Chronister (CEO) shown with 2 direct reports, one of whom (a Marketing Manager) has 1 direct report.
- Best-supported estimate: a small team, likely single digits to low teens, despite the multi-city, "award-winning" marketing positioning. Treat the multi-market presence as chiefly a content/SEO footprint rather than evidence of a large staffed organization.

## Why this matters for fit

- Enleaf is a genuine, active web design/marketing agency in the target geography (Spokane + confirmed CDA office) with a small team — structurally similar in size to other target leads.
- However, two factors move it further from the ideal profile than a fresh, growing freelancer/micro-shop like 509 Designs:
  1. It already sells hosting and maintenance in-house as an active, marketed offering (not a legacy/passive one) — real channel conflict for a pure referral pitch.
  2. It is a long-established (17+ year), "award-winning" agency with a highly public, book-publishing/speaking-circuit CEO — more likely to have settled vendor relationships and a busier, harder-to-reach principal than a smaller or newer shop.
- This doesn't rule the lead out, but it changes the right angle: white-label/Agency Hosting (per PROJECT.md's optional commercial-terms section) rather than a standard "send us clients you don't want to support" referral pitch.
