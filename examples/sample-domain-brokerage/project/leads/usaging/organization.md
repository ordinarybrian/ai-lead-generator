# USAging

## Organization

- Organization: USAging (formerly n4a — the National Association of Area Agencies on Aging)
- Website: https://www.usaging.org/
- Organization type: National nonprofit membership association. USAging is not a consumer-facing company — it represents and supports the national network of Area Agencies on Aging (AAAs) and advocates for the Title VI Native American Aging Programs. It has represented the AAA network for nearly 50 years and has advocated for Title VI programs since 1988.
- Relevant audience: USAging's direct members are the roughly 600+ local and regional Area Agencies on Aging and Title VI Native American Aging programs across the country — the organizations that actually plan, coordinate, and deliver home- and community-based services (meals, transportation, caregiver support, case management, senior centers, and more) at the local level. USAging's own end beneficiaries, one layer removed, are older adults, people with disabilities, and family caregivers nationwide.

## Relevant facts for positioning

- USAging administers the Eldercare Locator (https://eldercare.acl.gov, 1-800-677-1116) on behalf of the U.S. Administration on Aging, part of the federal Administration for Community Living (ACL). Launched in 1991, it is described as the only national information-and-referral resource covering the full range of issues affecting older Americans — housing, transportation, benefits eligibility, home- and community-based services, and more.
- The Eldercare Locator receives roughly 400,000 requests for assistance per year and its certified information specialists refer callers/chatters to more than 600 Area Agencies on Aging and nearly 300 Title VI Native American Aging Programs nationwide.
- In short: USAging already operates the closest real-world equivalent of what SeniorCenter.com is positioned to be — a national, federally-backed referral service connecting individuals to local aging services. It is delivered today primarily as a phone line/call center and a government-branded (.gov, ACL-run) website rather than as a standalone, consumer-friendly branded web destination.

## Potential SeniorCenter.com use

Because USAging already runs the Eldercare Locator, the plausible use case here is different from a company building a directory from scratch:

- SeniorCenter.com could serve as a more intuitive, brandable, consumer-facing front door that complements the Eldercare Locator's federally-run referral backend — something easier for the public to find and remember than eldercare.acl.gov, potentially focused specifically on senior centers and local community programming (a subset of what the Locator covers).
- It could function as a public awareness/marketing property USAging uses to drive more traffic and public recognition toward the AAA network and its member agencies, many of which directly operate or fund local senior centers.
- It is also plausible that USAging sees a public, non-federal domain as complementary bandwidth for content Eldercare Locator's government mandate doesn't emphasize (community events, classes, volunteer opportunities) rather than a core referral tool.

This is a working strategic hypothesis based on USAging's public mission and its existing Eldercare Locator program. It is not a claim about USAging's current plans, budget, or interest in acquiring a domain.

## Caution on organizational fit

USAging is a policy, advocacy, and membership-support association, not a marketing-driven consumer business — its budget priorities are member services, federal grant administration (it runs the Eldercare Locator under an ACL contract/cooperative agreement), and advocacy, not consumer acquisition spend. A $75,000–$149,000 domain purchase is a much bigger relative budget decision for an organization like this than for a national insurer, and there is no evidence of unrestricted marketing budget for this kind of acquisition. This should shape realistic expectations for the outreach, not the org's mission fit, which is strong.
